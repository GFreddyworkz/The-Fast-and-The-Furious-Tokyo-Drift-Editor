The in-game shop decides which parts to offer from a model number stored in each car. Pick a model and apply it to the car selected at the top, and the shop will offer that model’s parts. Experimental.

Tune shop blocked? (experimental)

The game refuses the tune shop for prize and dealer-tuned cars. These two tools are guesses, so try one car and tell me what the shop does. (1) Dealer-tuned cars have no tuning catalog stored: this gives them an estimated one. (2) Cars also carry a model key that the game may use for its compatibility lists: this swaps it. Neither changes how the car looks.

What works

  • Performance: every upgrade slot, levels 1 to 5, add or remove. All 371 upgrade records in the test saves rebuild byte for byte.
  • Visual: every part now shows its brand (Veilside, C-West, Spoon and so on), the car it was made for, the style number and the shop price. 14 body part slots (body kit, bonnet, bumpers, skirts, spoiler, trunk, lights, mirrors, windows, exhaust tip, neon, charm) with 2,339 parts from every car. All 508 part records in the test saves rebuild byte for byte. Not yet tested in the game.
  • Engines: the 14 aftermarket swaps are tested in the game. Stock engines have no item number in the game, so each is stored under a swap engine’s number. The game also keeps a list per car of which swaps it may use. 87 of the 125 models forbid every swap (the Mazdaspeed 6 is one), so the game will probably remove the engine there; the Engine tab now warns you and picks a swap the car is allowed to use when there is one.
  • Wheels: 203 wheels: Stock (102), Aftermarket (98), Unused (3). Wheel colour is a separate block the car already has; the editor keeps it, so a car with no colour block (like the Levin MR-S) shows the wheel’s default colour.
  • Rim and tyre size: front and rear, any size. The bytes this writes are identical to what the game wrote when you set 17 in / 255 mm. Sizes outside the shop’s range are untested.
  • Driver: money (matched against a car sale in two of your saves), plus profile name and completion shown read only.
  • Garage: clone a car, add any car from the game’s stock list, import a car from another save, or remove one. The add and remove steps reproduce the game’s own before/after saves (bought and sold Supra, sold 180SX) apart from a few counters.
  • Unlocks: cars, upgrades, stickers and shop discounts. 27 of the 37 cars are dealership cars; the other 10 are rival or story cars no dealer sells.
  • Earlier I called some cars “locked layout”. That was my mistake: every car uses the same layout, and all of them can be edited.

Limits

  • The shop only offers parts a car is allowed. Parts marked with a warning load fine, but the shop may remove them when you buy something else.
  • Not done yet: paint colours, wins and losses.
